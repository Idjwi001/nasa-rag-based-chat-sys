### Q1
Category: overview
Mission: apollo_11
Question: What was the main objective of Apollo 11, and was it achieved?
Expected: The objective was to land astronauts on the Moon and return them safely to Earth,
meeting the national commitment made on May 25, 1961. Apollo 11 launched from Kennedy Space
Center on July 16, 1969 with Neil Armstrong (Commander), Michael Collins (Command Module Pilot)
and Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin (Lunar Module Pilot). The lunar module Eagle landed in the Sea of
Tranquility at 102:45:40 GET on July 20, 1969 ("Houston, Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has
landed."). Armstrong first stepped onto the surface at 109:24:15 GET, and the crew returned
safely, so the objective was achieved.

### Q2
Category: emergency
Mission: apollo_13
Question: What problem did Apollo 13 encounter, and how did the crew first report it?
Expected: At about 55:55 GET (02 07 55 in the technical air-to-ground transcript) the crew
heard a loud bang and reported "Houston, we've had a problem," followed by a MAIN B BUS
UNDERVOLT caution. The cause was the failure of oxygen tank no. 2 in the service module,
which cut off the command module's oxygen and fuel-cell power. The lunar landing was
cancelled, and the crew used the lunar module as a lifeboat to get home.

### Q3
Category: technical
Mission: apollo_13
Question: How did the Apollo 13 crew deal with rising carbon dioxide levels while living in the lunar module?
Expected: The lunar module's own lithium hydroxide (LiOH) canisters could not remove carbon
dioxide for three crewmen for the whole return trip, and the command module's square LiOH
canisters did not fit the LM system. Mission Control read up a procedure for modifying a
command module LiOH canister, using hoses, a plastic bag and tape, so it could be attached
to the LM's suit hoses. With the improvised canisters the CO2 level came back down.

### Q4
Category: crew
Mission: challenger
Question: Who were the crew members of the Challenger STS-51L mission?
Expected: According to the launch-day commentary, the seven crew members were commander
Francis "Dick" Scobee, pilot Michael Smith, mission specialists Judith Resnik, Ellison
Onizuka and Ronald McNair, payload specialist Gregory Jarvis, and teacher-in-space
participant Christa McAuliffe, the first private citizen to fly on the shuttle.

### Q5
Category: disaster analysis
Mission: challenger
Question: What happened shortly after Challenger's launch, according to the mission audio?
Expected: The main engines throttled down to about 65% and then back up to 104%, and
Challenger was told "go at throttle up." Shortly after, at about 1 minute 15 seconds into
flight (velocity about 2,900 ft/s, altitude about 9 nautical miles), the public affairs
commentator said "Obviously a major malfunction." There was no downlink, the flight dynamics
officer reported that the vehicle had exploded, and contingency procedures were put into
effect. The transcripts do not explain the technical cause. The answer should say so, or
clearly mark the solid rocket booster O-ring failure as general knowledge rather than
archive content.

### Q6
Category: technical
Mission: apollo_11
Question: What was the 1202 program alarm during the Apollo 11 lunar descent, and how did Mission Control respond?
Expected: During powered descent the crew reported "PROGRAM ALARM ... It's a 1202" and asked
for a reading. Mission Control answered "We're GO on that alarm," and the descent continued.
The mission report explains that 1202 was an Executive overflow alarm. Unneeded counter
interrupts from the rendezvous radar coupling data unit used up computer time, so some
scheduled routines could not finish and the computer did a software restart. The report
says the alarms showed the guidance computer was working correctly, and that the radar
interrupts would not be processed by the program used on later missions.

### Q7
Category: timeline
Mission: apollo_13
Question: What was the sequence of key events for Apollo 13 after the accident, through splashdown?
Expected: After the oxygen tank failure the command module was powered down, and the crew
moved into the lunar module, using it as a lifeboat. Burns of the lunar module descent
engine put the spacecraft back on a trajectory to Earth around the Moon, and later midcourse
corrections refined it. Near Earth the crew jettisoned the service module, powered up the
command module and jettisoned the lunar module. Public affairs commentary gave the
predicted entry times: entry interface at about 142:40:40 GET, drogue chutes at 142:48:53,
main chutes at 142:49:43, and splashdown at about 142:54:40 GET in the Pacific.
